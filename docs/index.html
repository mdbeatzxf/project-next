Problem → Solution

What barbers, hairdressers and their clients struggle with today, as stated in the kickoff meeting, and what the app does about each point.

ProblemMany barbershops and hairdressers (especially African and Turkish barbers in German cities, and mobile braiders who work from home or come to you) have no booking structure: personal phone numbers, WhatsApp or Instagram DMs, cash, no fixed slots.
What we doA Provider profile with services, prices, opening hours and stylists, plus a simple calendar in the Provider dashboard. Clients book a slot in the app; the Provider confirms or declines. No phone number needed on either side.
ProblemClients walk in and do not know how long they will wait or how many people are ahead of them.
What we doThe walk-in queue: join remotely, get a position, see "3 people ahead, ~35 min", get a push when it is nearly your turn. The Provider manages the same queue live with Next / Skip / Done.
ProblemClients, especially Gen Z, do not want to hand out their phone number to book a haircut.
What we doNo phone numbers are exchanged. All communication runs through in-app notifications and messages; contact data stays private on the Platform.
ProblemClients new in a city do not know where to find a barber who can do their hair type or style ("I want an African hairdresser, where can I go?").
What we doDiscovery by city or address with filters for specialty (African hair, Turkish barber, braids, locs, extensions, beard, women's / men's, kids), "comes to your place" vs "you go there", price range and open now. Portfolio photos show the work.
ProblemExisting platforms (Check24, eBay Kleinanzeigen, Google, Facebook Marketplace, generic booking tools) do not serve this niche; barbers do not use them.
What we doNiche first: built only for barbers and hairdressers, seeded city by city through the barber network. Trust comes from portfolio, reviews and a verification badge, not from a generic marketplace.
ProblemMany barbers are not formally registered. Forcing online payment or invoicing would keep them away.
What we doCash is the default and stays the default. Online payment (PayPal, bank transfer, in-app) is optional, comes later and is never required. The Platform never issues invoices on the Provider's behalf.

Who is who

Three parties. The Provider is in the middle: our customer, and the one whose customers we help them serve.

Platform

Us: Marcus, Alex, Naayi. Admin side.

Verifies providers, moderates photos and reviews, support, metrics, pricing config.

verifies, hosts, supports pays a fee (later), brings clients

Provider

A barbershop, a hair salon, or an independent / mobile stylist (barber, braider, hairdresser). Type: shop · independent · mobile.

has Stylists Stylist · Murat Stylist · Deniz … 1 to n
confirms bookings, calls next, cuts hair books, joins queue, pays cash, reviews

Client

The person who gets the haircut. Finds a Provider, books or joins the queue, pays at the chair, leaves a review.

Provider = the platform's customer. Client = the provider's customer.

MVP vs later

v0.1 is the smallest thing a pilot barber can run a day with. Everything else waits.

MVP v0.1

  • Provider profile + services / prices + portfolio
  • Client discovery by city + specialty
  • Simple appointment booking (no payment)
  • Walk-in queue with "people ahead of me"
  • Notifications (push / email)
  • Provider dashboard (calendar + queue)
  • Reviews after a done booking
  • Admin verification

Later after v0.1

  • Online payment (PayPal, bank transfer, in-app; always optional)
  • Multi-stylist scheduling optimisation
  • Loyalty
  • Product sales (hair extensions / colour ordering idea from the meeting)
  • Other service categories
  • Other countries
  • Native apps
  • WhatsApp integration

Open questions

Nothing here is decided. Bring answers to the next meeting or open an issue.

  • Q1Name / brand, legal form, who owns what, time budget per person. All three have other commitments; Marcus said this cannot be pulled off alone.
  • Q2Build fresh or reuse Alex's existing marketplace code (Service Scout)?
  • Q3First cities, first 5–10 pilot providers, survey questions for barbers. A first vendor contact exists (a hairdresser friend); use the first vendors to validate.
  • Q4Pricing model and when to switch from free to paid. Options discussed: small fee per booking (a haircut is ~10–25 €), later a subscription for shops, premium placement.
  • Q5Legal: GDPR, Impressum, terms, handling providers who are not registered businesses, review law.
  • Q6Tech stack. Proposal above and in ADR-0001; to be decided at the next meeting.
